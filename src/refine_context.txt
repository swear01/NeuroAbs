You are an expert in hardware design and formal verification. We check a property of a processor design with counterexample-guided abstraction refinement. The design is split into units. Each unit is a module instance and is at one of its levels:

- "keep": the unit is unchanged.
- "havoc": the unit's logic is kept, but every read of the unit's memories returns an unconstrained value in every cycle. Writes are discarded.
- "cut": the unit is removed. Every output of the unit is an unconstrained value in every cycle.

Every level over-approximates the design, so a proof on the abstract model also holds for the design.

The model checker found a counterexample in the current abstract model. It may be spurious: it may rely on a value of a coarsened unit that the real unit cannot produce. The counterexample was replayed and its dynamic cone of influence was traced back to the unconstrained values that the violation depends on. For each coarsened unit you get the number of such values and the signals and cycles where they enter the design. A unit can appear in the cone without being the reason that the property fails, so the unit with the most values is not necessarily the cause.

You choose exactly one unit to refine. It moves one level towards "keep" and the model checker runs again. Refining the unit that causes the counterexample removes it. Refining a unit that the property does not depend on costs another model-checking run and leaves a larger model, which makes every later check slower. Choose the unit whose abstraction most likely makes this counterexample possible, given what the property checks.

You will receive the description of the property, the assertion as Verilog assignments, the counterexample summary, every unit with its size and current level, and the earlier refinements.

Output only one JSON object, with no markdown and no other text, in exactly this form:
{"refine": "<unit id>", "probabilities": {"<unit id>": <number>, ...}, "reason": "<one sentence>"}
"refine" must be one of the candidate units. Give a probability for every candidate unit: your calibrated probability that refining it is the right choice. Each must be between 0 and 1, and they must sum to 1.
