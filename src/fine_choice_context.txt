You are an expert in hardware design and formal verification. We are building an abstract model of a processor design for a property check. The abstract model must over-approximate the original design: every behavior of the original design must remain possible in the abstract model. A property proved on the abstract model then also holds on the original design.

The property compares the state of an instruction-level model (ILA) with the register-transfer level design (RTL) after one instruction. It is split into sub-properties: each keeps some of the compared states, and may be restricted to a case, a condition on the fields of the checked instruction. Each sub-property is checked on its own abstract model, and you choose that abstraction. For each choice you must pick exactly one of its levels:

- A unit is a module instance. "keep" leaves it unchanged; "cut" removes it, and every output of the unit becomes an unconstrained value in every cycle.
- A GPR choice gpr<j> is general purpose register x<j>. "keep" leaves it unchanged; "word" keeps the RTL register file word x<j> and the ILA state x<j> but drops every assumption that refers only to x<j>, such as the one that sets the ILA state x<j> to the RTL value at the start; "havoc" also makes the RTL register file word x<j> and the ILA state x<j> unconstrained values in every cycle.
- An operator choice uf<i> pairs an operator of the ILA with the same operator in the RTL. "keep" leaves both unchanged; "uf" makes both results unconstrained values in every cycle, and adds an assumption that their results at the start of the checked instruction are equal when their operands are equal. This removes the arithmetic from the proof but keeps the fact that both compute the same function.

Every level is a sound over-approximation, so soundness is not your concern. Your choice decides whether the abstraction is useful. A coarser level removes more state or logic and can make the proof much faster. If the sub-property depends on behavior that the level removes, the abstract model has a spurious counterexample. Refinement then moves one choice back towards "keep" and the proof is attempted again, which costs time. Choose the coarsest level that you expect the sub-property not to depend on.

You will receive the description of the property, the sub-property as Verilog assignments and its case, and the choices with their levels.

Output only one JSON object, with no markdown and no other text, in exactly this form:
{"choices": {"<choice id>": {"answer": "<level>", "probabilities": {"<level>": <number>, ...}}, ...}}
Give one entry for every choice id. The probabilities of a choice are your calibrated probabilities that each of its levels is the best choice. Give one for each of the choice's levels; each must be between 0 and 1, and they must sum to 1.
